📘 Grammar Master

Present Continuous: use it for actions happening now, around now, or temporary situations. Form: am/is/are + verb-ing.

I am studying now.

Think: NOW.

Present Simple: use it for habits, routines, repeated actions, facts, and regular schedules.

I study English every Monday.

Think: USUALLY / EVERY DAY.

Affirmative Present Continuous: I am, you are, he/she/it is, we/they are + verb-ing.

They are playing football.

The auxiliary changes; the main verb gets -ing.

Negative Present Continuous: am not, isn't, aren't + verb-ing.

He isn't sleeping.

Do not use do/does with Present Continuous negatives.

Questions in Present Continuous: Am/Is/Are + subject + verb-ing?

Are you studying?

The auxiliary comes before the subject.

Present Simple negative: don't/doesn't + base verb.

She doesn't wear a uniform.

After doesn't, use the base verb: doesn't wear, not doesn't wears.

Present Simple questions: Do/Does + subject + base verb?

Does he play football?

After does, use the base verb.

Time markers: now, right now, at the moment usually signal Present Continuous.

She is working right now.

These markers help you choose the tense.

Habit markers: always, usually, often, sometimes, never, every day often signal Present Simple.

He usually wears jeans.

Frequency words describe routines.

Spelling -ing: most verbs simply add -ing.

play → playing

Do not change the spelling unnecessarily.

Verbs ending in silent -e usually drop the e before -ing.

write → writing

make → making; use → using.

Short vowel + consonant in many one-syllable verbs can double the final consonant.

run → running

sit → sitting; swim → swimming.

Verb ending in -ie changes to -y before -ing.

lie → lying

die → dying.

Some verbs are commonly used in the Simple rather than Continuous when describing states.

I know the answer.

Keep the lesson focused on the contrast taught here.
